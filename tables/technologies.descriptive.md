# Technologies

What each technology in `technologies.tsv` is, in real-world terms, grouped by knowledge domain (`domains.tsv`) and research area (`areas.tsv`). The grouping is for display and for allocating research; it says nothing about how technologies behave or bear on one another. Speculative technologies are described as their proponents imagine them.

## Mathematics

Pure and applied mathematics, logic and statistics: the formal sciences.

### Foundations

Logic, sets, categories and types: what mathematics is built on.

- **Mathematical Logic** — Formal systems, proof, and the limits of what can be proved.
- **Set Theory** — Sets and infinities: the standard foundation of mathematics.
- **Category Theory** — Mathematics of structure and the maps between structures.
- **Type Theory** — Formal systems of types, a foundation for both proofs and programs.
- **Model Theory** — The relation between formal theories and the structures that satisfy them.

### Algebra and Number Theory

Numbers, equations and abstract algebraic structure.

- **Number Theory** — Properties of the integers and primes.
- **Abstract Algebra** — Groups, rings, fields and modules in general.
- **Group Theory** — The mathematics of symmetry.
- **Linear Algebra** — Vectors, matrices and linear maps, the workhorse of applied mathematics.
- **Representation Theory** — Abstract symmetries studied through the matrices that act them out.
- **Algebraic Geometry** — Geometry of the solutions of polynomial equations.
- **Langlands Program** — A web of conjectures unifying number theory, geometry and representation theory.

### Geometry and Topology

Shape, space and the properties that survive deformation.

- **Differential Geometry** — Curved spaces and the calculus on them.
- **Topology** — Properties of spaces preserved under continuous deformation.
- **Algebraic Topology** — Classifying spaces by algebraic invariants.
- **Knot Theory** — Mathematical knots and their invariants.
- **Fractal Geometry** — Self-similar shapes with fractional dimension.
- **Noncommutative Geometry** — Geometry of spaces whose coordinates do not commute.

### Analysis

Limits, change and the infinite: calculus and its descendants.

- **Calculus** — Derivatives and integrals: the mathematics of continuous change.
- **Complex Analysis** — Calculus of functions of complex numbers.
- **Functional Analysis** — Infinite-dimensional spaces of functions and operators on them.
- **Differential Equations** — Equations relating quantities to their rates of change.
- **Harmonic Analysis** — Functions as sums of waves: Fourier analysis and its generalizations.
- **Dynamical Systems** — How systems evolve in time: stability, cycles and attractors.
- **Chaos Theory** — Deterministic systems whose outcomes are exquisitely sensitive to their start.

### Discrete Mathematics

Counting, graphs and codes: the mathematics of separate things.

- **Combinatorics** — Counting and arranging finite structures.
- **Graph Theory** — Networks of nodes and links in the abstract.
- **Coding Theory** — Codes that detect and correct errors in stored and transmitted data.

### Probability and Statistics

Chance, inference and learning from data.

- **Probability Theory** — The mathematics of chance.
- **Statistics** — Drawing conclusions from data under uncertainty.
- **Bayesian Inference** — Updating beliefs by evidence through Bayes' rule.
- **Stochastic Processes** — Random quantities evolving in time.
- **Causal Inference** — Telling cause from correlation, in experiments and observational data.

### Applied Mathematics

Mathematics made to model, compute, optimize and decide.

- **Numerical Analysis** — Algorithms that compute approximate answers to continuous problems.
- **Optimization** — Finding the best choice under constraints.
- **Control Theory** — Steering dynamic systems by feedback.
- **Operations Research** — Mathematical methods for running organizations: scheduling, queues, logistics.
- **Game Theory** — Strategic interaction between rational decision makers.
- **Decision Theory** — Rational choice under uncertainty.
- **Network Science** — Structure and dynamics of real networks: social, biological, technological.
- **Complex Systems** — Emergent behavior of many interacting parts.

## Information

Computing, artificial intelligence, communications and cryptography: how information is processed, moved and protected.

### Computer Science

The theory and practice of computation and software.

- **Theory of Computation** — What can be computed, and at what cost: computability and complexity.
- **Algorithms** — Step-by-step procedures for solving problems efficiently.
- **Programming Languages** — Languages, compilers and the semantics of programs.
- **Software Engineering** — Building and maintaining large software systems.
- **Databases** — Storing, organizing and querying data at scale.
- **Distributed Computing** — Many computers working on one problem, tolerating failure and delay.
- **Cloud Computing** — Hyperscale data centers and the computing they rent out.
- **Formal Verification** — Proving mathematically that hardware and software do what they should.
- **Human-Computer Interaction** — How people and computers work together.
- **Computer Graphics** — Rendering images and worlds by computer.
- **Data Science** — Extracting knowledge from large and messy data.
- **Information Theory** — Quantifying information: entropy, channel capacity and compression.

### Artificial Intelligence

Machines that learn, perceive, reason and act.

- **Machine Learning** — Algorithms that improve from data rather than explicit rules.
- **Deep Learning** — Learning with many-layered neural networks.
- **Reinforcement Learning** — Learning to act by trial, error and reward.
- **Large Language Models** — Neural networks trained on vast text to understand and generate language.
- **Computer Vision** — Machines that see: recognizing objects, scenes and motion.
- **Natural Language Processing** — Machines that read, write, listen and speak.
- **Knowledge Representation** — Encoding facts and concepts so machines can reason with them.
- **Automated Reasoning** — Machines that prove theorems and solve problems by deduction.
- **Autonomous Agents** — AI systems that pursue goals on their own over many steps.
- **Affective Computing** — Machines that recognize, interpret and express emotion.
- **Evolutionary Computation** — Search and design by simulated evolution.
- **AI Alignment** — Making AI systems pursue the goals their makers intend.
- **Interpretability** — Understanding what happens inside trained neural networks.
- **Artificial General Intelligence** — AI that matches people across the whole range of cognitive work.
- **Superintelligence** — AI far beyond the best human minds in every domain.

### Computing Hardware

Processors, memory and displays, and new ways to compute.

- **Processor Design** — Architecture and design of microprocessors and their circuits.
- **AI Accelerators** — Tensor and graphics processors built for neural-network arithmetic.
- **High-Performance Computing** — Supercomputers and the science of using them.
- **Data Storage** — Memory and storage media, from chips to disks to archives.
- **Displays** — Screens and the light-emitting devices that make them.
- **Neuromorphic Computing** — Hardware that computes as brains do, with spiking neurons and synapses.
- **Quantum Computing** — Computers that exploit superposition and entanglement.
- **Photonic Computing** — Computing with light instead of electrons.
- **Reversible Computing** — Logic that erases nothing, to compute near the thermodynamic limit.
- **Biocomputing** — Computing and storing data with DNA, proteins and living cells.
- **Computronium** — Matter arranged to compute as much as physics allows, up to brains the size of stars.

### Communications

Moving information across wires, air, light and space.

- **Signal Processing** — Analyzing, filtering and transforming signals.
- **Telecommunications** — Carrying voice, video and data over distance.
- **Computer Networking** — The internet and the protocols that knit computers together.
- **Wireless Communications** — Radio links: mobile networks, Wi-Fi and phased arrays.
- **Optical Communications** — Data carried by light in fibers and free space.
- **Satellite Communications** — Communications relayed by satellites in orbit.
- **Deep Space Communications** — Radio and laser links across interplanetary distances.
- **Delay-Tolerant Networking** — Networking that works across light-minutes of delay and broken links.
- **Quantum Communication** — Sending quantum states, for unbreakable keys and quantum networks.

### Cryptography and Security

Secrecy, trust and defense in the digital world.

- **Cryptography** — Codes and ciphers that keep information secret and authentic.
- **Post-Quantum Cryptography** — Cryptography that quantum computers cannot break.
- **Cybersecurity** — Defending computers and networks against attack.
- **Distributed Ledgers** — Blockchains and other shared records that no one party controls.
- **Zero-Knowledge Proofs** — Proving a statement true while revealing nothing else.
- **Privacy-Preserving Computation** — Computing on data that stays encrypted: homomorphic encryption and secure multiparty computation.

### Virtual Worlds

Simulated worlds, and immersion in them.

- **Computer Simulation** — Modeling physical and social systems on computers.
- **Digital Twins** — Live simulated copies of real plants, machines and cities.
- **Virtual Reality** — Immersive computer-generated worlds.
- **Augmented Reality** — Digital content laid over the real world.
- **Metaverse** — Persistent shared virtual worlds where people meet, work and play.
- **Telepresence** — Being present somewhere else through screens, sensors and robots.
- **Haptics** — Technology of touch: feeling virtual and remote objects.

## Physics

Matter, energy, space and time from the quantum to the cosmic, and the light and instruments that reveal them.

### Classical Physics

Mechanics, heat, fluids, fields and waves at human scales.

- **Classical Mechanics** — Motion of bodies under forces, from Newton to Hamilton.
- **Thermodynamics** — Heat, work, energy and entropy.
- **Statistical Mechanics** — Large-scale behavior of matter from the statistics of its parts.
- **Fluid Dynamics** — Flow of liquids and gases, turbulence included.
- **Electromagnetism** — Electric and magnetic fields, circuits, radio and light.
- **Acoustics** — Sound and vibration in every medium.
- **Continuum Mechanics** — Stress, strain and failure in solids treated as continuous media.
- **Cryogenics** — Producing and working at temperatures near absolute zero.

### Quantum Physics

The quantum world: its theory, its puzzles and its uses.

- **Quantum Mechanics** — The physics of atoms, light and every small thing.
- **Quantum Foundations** — What quantum theory means: measurement, reality and interpretation.
- **Quantum Field Theory** — Quantum theory of fields, the language of particle physics.
- **Quantum Information Science** — Information carried and processed by quantum states.
- **Quantum Optics** — Light and matter at the level of single photons.
- **Quantum Sensing** — Measuring fields, time and motion with quantum states.

### Particles and Fields

The fundamental particles and forces, and the theories that would unify them.

- **Particle Physics** — The Standard Model and the search for what lies beyond it.
- **Neutrino Physics** — The elusive neutrinos: their masses, mixing and sources.
- **Particle Accelerators** — Machines that drive particles to high energy, for collisions and light.
- **Supersymmetry** — A proposed symmetry between matter and force particles, so far unseen.
- **String Theory** — Particles as vibrating strings, a candidate theory of everything.
- **Quantum Gravity** — Reconciling gravity with quantum theory.
- **Loop Quantum Gravity** — Spacetime quantized as a network of loops, the main rival to strings.
- **Antimatter** — Making, cooling and storing antiparticles.

### Gravitation

Gravity, spacetime and black holes, and what might one day be done with them.

- **General Relativity** — Gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
- **Gravitational Waves** — Detecting ripples in spacetime from colliding black holes and stars.
- **Black Hole Physics** — Black holes: their horizons, radiation and information.
- **Modified Gravity** — Alternatives to general relativity, proposed to explain dark matter or dark energy.
- **Spacetime Engineering** — Shaping spacetime itself: warp metrics, traversable wormholes and the exotic matter they would need.
- **Black Hole Engineering** — Drawing power from black holes, or making small ones.

### Condensed Matter

Solids, liquids and exotic phases of matter.

- **Solid-State Physics** — Electrons, lattices and magnetism in crystals.
- **Semiconductor Physics** — Electrons and holes in semiconductors, and the devices they make.
- **Superconductivity** — Electrical resistance that vanishes, and the hunt for it at higher temperatures.
- **Topological Matter** — Phases of matter protected by topology: topological insulators and their kin.
- **Soft Matter** — Polymers, gels, foams, colloids and liquid crystals.
- **Spintronics** — Electronics using the spin of electrons as well as their charge.

### Nuclear and Plasma Physics

Atomic nuclei, and matter hot enough to strip its electrons.

- **Nuclear Physics** — Structure of nuclei, radioactivity, fission and fusion.
- **Plasma Physics** — Ionized gases, from fusion reactors to the solar wind.

### Atoms and Light

Atoms and light, lasers and photonics, and the art of exact measurement.

- **Atomic and Molecular Physics** — Structure and behavior of atoms and molecules.
- **Optics** — Lenses, mirrors and the behavior of light.
- **Lasers** — Coherent light sources, from chips to petawatts.
- **Photonics** — Generating, guiding and detecting light on chips and in fibers.
- **Ultracold Atoms** — Atoms cooled to billionths of a degree: Bose-Einstein condensates and quantum simulators.
- **Precision Metrology** — Exact measurement: atomic clocks, interferometers and electron microscopes.

## Chemistry

Molecules and their reactions, from the bench to the plants that make chemicals by the million tonnes.

### Chemical Science

The science of molecules, their structure and their reactions.

- **Physical Chemistry** — Physics of chemical systems: bonding, thermodynamics and kinetics.
- **Organic Chemistry** — Carbon compounds and how to make them.
- **Inorganic Chemistry** — Metals, minerals and every compound not built on carbon.
- **Analytical Chemistry** — Identifying and measuring what substances contain: spectroscopy, chromatography, mass spectrometry.
- **Computational Chemistry** — Predicting molecules and reactions by computer.
- **Electrochemistry** — Chemistry driven by and driving electric current.
- **Photochemistry** — Reactions driven by light.
- **Radiochemistry** — Chemistry of radioactive elements.
- **Laboratory Automation** — Robotic laboratories, directed by AI, that plan, run and interpret experiments.

### Chemical Engineering

Turning chemistry into processes and plants.

- **Catalysis** — Speeding and steering reactions with catalysts.
- **Process Engineering** — Designing and running chemical plants.
- **Separation Science** — Separating mixtures: distillation, membranes, sorbents and centrifuges.
- **Polymer Chemistry** — Making and understanding plastics, fibers and other long-chain molecules.
- **Petrochemistry** — Refining oil and gas into fuels and chemical feedstocks.
- **Green Chemistry** — Chemistry that makes less waste, from benign and renewable inputs.
- **Supramolecular Chemistry** — Chemistry beyond the molecule: assemblies held by weak bonds.
- **Nitrogen Fixation** — Turning air's nitrogen into ammonia and fertilizer, in plants and in microbes.

## Materials

The science and engineering of materials, from alloys and ceramics to nanomaterials and matter designed atom by atom.

### Materials Science

How the structure of a material sets what it can do.

- **Materials Science** — Relating the structure of materials to their properties, and designing new ones.
- **Metallurgy** — Alloys and the processing that gives metals their properties.
- **Superalloys** — Alloys that keep their strength near their melting point, for turbines and engines.
- **High-Entropy Alloys** — Alloys of many elements in near-equal parts, with unusual strength and toughness.
- **Ceramics and Glass** — Hard, heat-resistant inorganic materials, and glass in all its forms.
- **Composite Materials** — Materials of fibers in a matrix, such as carbon fiber.
- **Biomaterials** — Materials that work with living tissue, and materials made by living things.
- **Materials Informatics** — Discovering materials with data, simulation and machine learning.
- **Superconducting Materials** — Superconducting wire, tape and magnets, and the search for them at room temperature.
- **Smart Materials** — Materials that change shape or properties on command: shape-memory, piezoelectric, electroactive.
- **Self-Healing Materials** — Materials that repair their own damage.
- **Radiation-Resistant Materials** — Materials that endure neutrons and cosmic rays, for reactors and spacecraft.
- **Thermal Protection** — Heat shields and hot structures for reentry and hypersonic flight.
- **Metallic Hydrogen** — Hydrogen crushed into a metal, perhaps a stable superconductor and the densest rocket fuel.
- **Synthetic Diamond** — Growing diamond for tools, optics, electronics and gems.

### Nanotechnology

Engineering at the scale of molecules.

- **Nanotechnology** — Making and using structures a few billionths of a meter across.
- **Two-Dimensional Materials** — Graphene and other materials one atom thick.
- **Carbon Nanotubes** — Rolled sheets of carbon, the strongest fibers known.
- **Metamaterials** — Structures that bend light, sound and radio in ways no natural material can.
- **Self-Assembly** — Parts that organize themselves into structures, as DNA origami does.
- **Molecular Machines** — Molecules built to move: motors, switches and pumps.
- **Molecular Manufacturing** — Building products by placing atoms where a design puts them.
- **Programmable Matter** — Matter that changes its shape and properties on command.

## Energy

Making, storing and moving energy: fossil, renewable, fission and fusion power, storage and grids.

### Fossil Energy

Extracting and burning coal, oil and gas.

- **Combustion Engineering** — Burning fuels efficiently and cleanly in engines, boilers and furnaces.
- **Gas Turbines** — Turbines for power plants and jet engines.
- **Oil and Gas Extraction** — Finding and producing oil and gas, onshore and offshore.
- **Hydraulic Fracturing** — Cracking tight rock with pressurized fluid to release oil, gas or heat.

### Renewable Energy

Power from sunlight, wind, water, Earth's heat and living things.

- **Photovoltaics** — Solar cells that turn light into electricity.
- **Concentrated Solar Power** — Mirrors focusing sunlight for heat and power.
- **Wind Turbines** — Turbines that turn wind into electricity, on land and at sea.
- **Hydropower** — Electricity from falling and flowing water.
- **Geothermal Energy** — Power and heat from hot rock and water underground.
- **Enhanced Geothermal** — Engineered geothermal reservoirs in hot dry rock, wherever drilling can reach.
- **Ocean Energy** — Power from tides, waves and ocean heat.
- **Bioenergy** — Fuel and power from biomass: biofuels, biogas and wood.
- **Space Solar Power** — Collecting sunlight in orbit and beaming the power down.

### Nuclear Fission

Power from splitting heavy nuclei, and its fuel and waste.

- **Fission Reactors** — Nuclear reactors for power, from today's water-cooled plants on.
- **Small Modular Reactors** — Reactors small enough to build in factories and ship whole.
- **Fast Reactors** — Reactors driven by fast neutrons, able to breed fuel and burn waste.
- **Molten Salt Reactors** — Reactors whose fuel is dissolved in molten salt, the thorium cycle among them.
- **Nuclear Fuel Cycle** — Mining, enriching, fabricating and reprocessing nuclear fuel.
- **Nuclear Waste Management** — Storing and disposing of spent fuel and radioactive waste.
- **Radioisotope Power** — Long-lived power from radioactive decay, for spacecraft and remote sites.

### Fusion

Power from joining light nuclei, the way the Sun makes it.

- **Magnetic Confinement** — Holding fusion plasma in magnetic fields: tokamaks and stellarators.
- **Inertial Confinement** — Fusion by crushing fuel pellets with lasers or pulsed power.
- **Magneto-Inertial Fusion** — Hybrid schemes that compress magnetized plasma.
- **Fusion Power** — Fusion plants that deliver net electricity: blankets, tritium breeding, remote maintenance.
- **Aneutronic Fusion** — Fusion of fuels such as deuterium with helium-3, or proton with boron, that release few neutrons.

### Storage and Grids

Storing energy, converting it between carriers, and moving it as electricity.

- **Lithium-Ion Batteries** — Rechargeable batteries for vehicles, devices and grids.
- **Solid-State Batteries** — Batteries with solid electrolytes, denser and safer.
- **Flow Batteries** — Batteries whose energy is stored in tanks of liquid, for the grid.
- **Thermal Energy Storage** — Storing energy as heat in salt, rock or water.
- **Water Electrolysis** — Splitting water into hydrogen and oxygen with electricity.
- **Hydrogen Storage** — Storing hydrogen as compressed gas, cryogenic liquid or in solids.
- **Fuel Cells** — Turning hydrogen and other fuels directly into electricity.
- **Synthetic Fuels** — Fuels made from electricity, water and carbon dioxide: hydrogen, methane, methanol and liquid fuels.
- **Power Electronics** — Converting and controlling electric power with semiconductors.
- **Power Transmission** — High-voltage lines, cables and the long-distance links of the grid.
- **Smart Grids** — Grids that sense, forecast and balance supply and demand by themselves.
- **Beamed Power** — Sending power over distance by laser or microwave beam.

## Industry

Making things at scale: the engineering disciplines, manufacturing, robotics, construction, mining and transport.

### Engineering Disciplines

The general engineering crafts every industry draws on.

- **Mechanical Engineering** — Designing machines and the parts that move them.
- **Electrical Engineering** — Motors, generators, power systems and machines that run on electricity.
- **Electronic Engineering** — Circuits and electronic systems.
- **Systems Engineering** — Designing and integrating complex systems as wholes.
- **Reliability Engineering** — Making systems that keep working, and predicting when they fail.
- **Human Factors** — Designing work, tools and machines around people.
- **Sensors** — Devices that measure the physical world: cameras, lidar, accelerometers and the rest.

### Manufacturing

Turning materials into products.

- **Machine Tools** — Precision machining: the machines that make machines.
- **Additive Manufacturing** — Building parts layer by layer: 3D printing in plastic, metal and more.
- **Semiconductor Fabrication** — Making chips: lithography, deposition, etching and the yield that pays for them.
- **Microfabrication** — Machines and channels on chips: MEMS sensors and microfluidics.
- **Industrial Automation** — Factories run by control systems, networks and robots.
- **Lean Manufacturing** — Production systems that eliminate waste and improve without end.
- **Electronics Manufacturing** — Assembling circuit boards, devices and electronic goods by the billion.
- **Textile Technology** — Spinning, weaving and finishing fibers, technical textiles included.

### Robotics

Machines that sense, move and act in the physical world.

- **Robotics** — Machines that sense, decide and move: the integration of mechanics, electronics and control.
- **Industrial Robots** — Robot arms and vehicles that weld, paint, pick and carry in factories and warehouses.
- **Dexterous Manipulation** — Robot hands that grasp and handle things as deftly as people do.
- **Legged Locomotion** — Robots that walk, run and climb on legs.
- **Humanoid Robots** — General-purpose robots in human form, able to work in places built for people.
- **Soft Robotics** — Robots of flexible materials, gentle and adaptable.
- **Swarm Robotics** — Many simple robots coordinating as one.
- **Telerobotics** — Robots operated by people at a distance.
- **Exoskeletons** — Powered suits that add strength and endurance to the wearer.
- **Self-Replicating Machines** — Factories that build copies of themselves from raw materials.

### Construction

Building structures, infrastructure and cities.

- **Civil Engineering** — Roads, bridges, dams, water systems and other infrastructure.
- **Structural Engineering** — Making buildings and structures stand up to their loads.
- **Geotechnical Engineering** — Building on and in soil and rock.
- **Tunneling** — Boring and building tunnels.
- **Construction Automation** — Robots and printers that build buildings.
- **Modular Construction** — Buildings made of factory-built modules.
- **Arcologies** — Whole cities in single dense, largely self-contained structures.

### Mining and Metals

Getting ores out of the ground and metals out of the ores.

- **Mining Engineering** — Planning and working mines, open-pit and underground.
- **Mineral Processing** — Crushing, sorting and concentrating ores.
- **Extractive Metallurgy** — Smelting and refining metals from their ores.
- **Steelmaking** — Making iron and steel, by blast furnace, electric arc or hydrogen.
- **Deep Drilling** — Drilling kilometers into the crust for oil, gas, heat and science.
- **Deep-Sea Mining** — Harvesting nodules and deposits from the deep ocean floor.
- **Biomining** — Extracting metals with microbes.

### Transport

Vehicles and systems that move people and goods on Earth.

- **Automotive Engineering** — Designing and building cars and trucks.
- **Electric Vehicles** — Cars, trucks and buses driven by batteries and electric motors.
- **Autonomous Vehicles** — Vehicles that drive themselves.
- **High-Speed Rail** — Trains at 250 to 350 km/h on dedicated lines.
- **Maglev** — Trains floated and driven by magnets.
- **Hyperloop** — Pods in near-vacuum tubes at airliner speeds.
- **Aeronautics** — Designing and building aircraft.
- **Electric Aviation** — Battery and hybrid aircraft, from air taxis to regional planes.
- **Hypersonic Flight** — Flight in the atmosphere above five times the speed of sound.
- **Naval Architecture** — Designing and building ships.
- **Logistics** — Moving and storing goods through supply chains.
- **Drones** — Uncrewed aircraft, from toys to cargo carriers.

## Space

The universe and the solar system, and going there: launch, propulsion, spacecraft, habitation, space resources and astroengineering.

### Astronomy

Observing the universe and explaining it.

- **Observational Astronomy** — Telescopes and surveys across the spectrum.
- **Radio Astronomy** — The sky in radio waves, from single dishes to planet-sized arrays.
- **Space Telescopes** — Observatories above the atmosphere.
- **Astrophysics** — The physics of stars, galaxies and everything between them.
- **Cosmology** — The origin, history and fate of the universe.
- **Dark Matter** — The unseen matter that holds galaxies together: what it is.
- **Dark Energy** — Whatever drives the accelerating expansion of the universe.
- **Exoplanet Science** — Finding planets around other stars and learning what they are like.
- **SETI** — The search for extraterrestrial intelligence: listening and looking for signs of technology beyond Earth.

### Planetary Science

The worlds of the solar system, their makeup and their resources.

- **Planetary Science** — Planets, moons and small bodies: how they formed and what they are made of.
- **Planetary Geology** — Rocks, ices and surfaces of other worlds.
- **Astrobiology** — Life in the universe: where it could arise, and how to find it.
- **Asteroid Science** — Asteroids and comets: their orbits, makeup and history.
- **Heliophysics** — The Sun and its reach through the solar system: solar wind, flares and space weather.
- **Space Resource Prospecting** — Finding and measuring minable deposits off Earth.

### Launch

Getting from a surface to orbit.

- **Rocketry** — Designing, building and flying rockets and their engines.
- **Reusable Rockets** — Rockets that land and fly again.
- **Super Heavy Lift** — Launchers that carry a hundred tonnes or more to orbit.
- **Spaceplanes** — Winged craft that fly to orbit, some breathing air on the way.
- **Mass Drivers** — Electromagnetic launchers, most at home on the airless Moon.
- **Skyhooks** — Rotating tethers that catch payloads from the upper atmosphere and fling them to orbit.
- **Launch Loops** — A moving magnetic belt held up by its own momentum, a track to orbit.
- **Space Elevators** — A cable from the equator to beyond geostationary orbit, climbed to space.

### In-Space Propulsion

Moving between orbits and worlds.

- **Cryogenic Fluid Management** — Keeping liquid hydrogen, oxygen and methane cold and usable in space for months.
- **Electric Propulsion** — Ion and Hall thrusters: high efficiency at low thrust.
- **Nuclear Thermal Propulsion** — Rockets that heat hydrogen in a fission reactor.
- **Nuclear Electric Propulsion** — Fission reactors powering electric thrusters.
- **Solar Sails** — Sails pushed by sunlight, needing no propellant.
- **Beamed Propulsion** — Sails pushed by lasers or microwaves from afar.
- **Fusion Propulsion** — Rockets driven by fusion, for fast travel across the solar system.
- **Nuclear Pulse Propulsion** — Ships pushed by a train of nuclear explosions behind them.
- **Antimatter Propulsion** — Rockets powered by matter meeting antimatter.
- **Warp Drive** — Moving a region of spacetime, and the ship within it, faster than light.
- **Electrodynamic Tethers** — Conducting tethers that push against a planet's magnetic field.

### Spacecraft

Building, flying and servicing the craft themselves.

- **Spacecraft Engineering** — Designing and building satellites, probes and crewed craft.
- **Astrodynamics** — Orbits and trajectories: how to get from here to there in space.
- **Guidance and Navigation** — Knowing where a craft is and steering it there: guidance, navigation, control, rendezvous and docking.
- **Orbital Refueling** — Transferring propellant between craft in orbit.
- **In-Space Assembly** — Building and servicing structures and craft in orbit, mostly by robot.
- **Entry and Landing** — Getting through an atmosphere and down to a surface intact.
- **Satellite Constellations** — Thousands of mass-produced satellites working as one network.
- **Planetary Rovers** — Robotic vehicles that explore other worlds.
- **Orbital Debris Mitigation** — Tracking, avoiding and removing space junk.

### Space Habitation

Keeping people alive and well off Earth.

- **Life Support Systems** — Air, water and waste systems that keep crews alive.
- **Bioregenerative Life Support** — Closed ecosystems that recycle air, water and food.
- **Radiation Shielding** — Protecting crews and electronics from cosmic rays and solar storms.
- **Artificial Gravity** — Spinning habitats and craft to stand in for gravity.
- **Space Habitats** — Stations and habitats in orbit and deep space, rigid and inflatable.
- **Extravehicular Activity** — Spacesuits and working outside the craft.
- **Surface Bases** — Bases on the Moon, Mars and other surfaces.
- **Space Farming** — Growing crops in space and on other worlds.
- **Space Medicine** — Keeping people healthy in weightlessness, radiation and isolation.

### Space Resources

Living off the land: mining, processing and building with what space provides.

- **In-Situ Resource Utilization** — Making propellant, water, oxygen and materials from local resources.
- **Volatiles Extraction** — Mining water ice and other volatiles from shadowed craters, regolith and comets.
- **Molten Regolith Electrolysis** — Melting regolith and splitting it into oxygen and metals with electricity.
- **Asteroid Mining** — Extracting metals and volatiles from asteroids.
- **Helium-3 Mining** — Gathering helium-3 from lunar regolith or giant planets as fusion fuel.
- **Space Manufacturing** — Making goods in orbit, where vacuum and weightlessness help.
- **Regolith Construction** — Building with sintered, printed or bagged regolith.

### Astroengineering

Engineering at the scale of worlds and stars, and the first steps to other stars.

- **O'Neill Cylinders** — Rotating space colonies kilometers long, with land, air and sky inside.
- **Orbital Rings** — A ring around a planet, held in place by a fast inner stream, with elevators down.
- **Terraforming** — Remaking a world's climate and chemistry to suit life.
- **Dyson Swarms** — Vast swarms of collectors gathering a star's light.
- **Star Lifting** — Drawing matter out of a star.
- **Stellar Engines** — Using a star's own output to move it, and its planets with it.
- **Interstellar Probes** — Craft that reach other stars within a human lifetime.
- **Generation Ships** — Worlds in motion whose crews live and die on the voyage to another star.

## Earth

The planet's rock, water, air and climate, and the engineering that manages them.

### Geoscience

The solid Earth, its waters and its resources.

- **Geology** — Rocks, minerals and the history of the Earth.
- **Geophysics** — The Earth's interior seen through seismic waves, gravity and magnetism.
- **Geochemistry** — The chemistry of rocks, waters and the cycles between them.
- **Mineral Exploration** — Finding ore bodies and other deposits worth mining.
- **Earth Observation** — Watching the planet from orbit and from the air.
- **Hydrology** — Water on and under the land: rivers, aquifers and floods.
- **Oceanography** — The oceans: their currents, chemistry and life.
- **Soil Science** — Soils: how they form, what lives in them and what they grow.

### Atmosphere and Climate

Weather, the air's chemistry and the climate system.

- **Meteorology** — Weather and its forecasting.
- **Climate Science** — The climate system and how it changes.
- **Atmospheric Chemistry** — The chemistry of the air: ozone, aerosols and pollutants.

### Environmental Engineering

Clean water, clean air, and what to do with waste.

- **Water Treatment** — Making water safe to drink and wastewater safe to release.
- **Desalination** — Fresh water from seawater and brine.
- **Pollution Control** — Scrubbers, filters and catalytic converters.
- **Waste Management** — Collecting, treating and disposing of waste.
- **Recycling** — Recovering materials from what is thrown away.
- **Environmental Remediation** — Cleaning contaminated land and water, by microbes among other means.

### Conservation and Restoration

Keeping and restoring living systems.

- **Conservation Biology** — Protecting species and habitats.
- **Ecosystem Restoration** — Rebuilding damaged ecosystems, and rewilding.
- **De-Extinction** — Bringing back lost species, or their nearest likeness.

### Geoengineering

Changing the climate and weather on purpose.

- **Carbon Capture and Storage** — Capturing carbon dioxide from plants and burying it.
- **Direct Air Capture** — Pulling carbon dioxide straight out of the air.
- **Enhanced Weathering** — Spreading crushed rock to lock carbon dioxide into minerals.
- **Solar Geoengineering** — Cooling a planet by reflecting sunlight, with aerosols or sunshades.
- **Weather Modification** — Changing the weather on purpose, as cloud seeding does.

## Life

Living things from molecules to ecosystems, the biotechnology that engineers them, and the agriculture that feeds us.

### Molecular Biology

The molecules of life and the genetic code.

- **Molecular Biology** — How DNA, RNA and proteins carry out the work of the cell.
- **Biochemistry** — The chemistry of living things.
- **Structural Biology** — The shapes of biological molecules, and how shape sets function.
- **Genetics** — Heredity and variation.
- **Genomics** — Reading whole genomes, cheaply and at scale.
- **Epigenetics** — Heritable changes in how genes are used, without changes to the genes.

### Organismal Biology

Cells, bodies and the kingdoms of life.

- **Cell Biology** — How cells are built and how they work.
- **Developmental Biology** — How one cell becomes a whole organism.
- **Physiology** — How bodies work, organ by organ.
- **Microbiology** — Bacteria, archaea, fungi and other microbes.
- **Virology** — Viruses and how they infect.
- **Immunology** — The immune system, in health and disease.
- **Plant Biology** — How plants grow, photosynthesize and defend themselves.
- **Zoology** — Animals: their bodies, behavior and diversity.
- **Mycology** — Fungi, from yeasts to mycelial networks.

### Evolution and Ecology

Life's history, its systems and its webs.

- **Evolutionary Biology** — How life evolves.
- **Ecology** — How organisms interact with one another and their surroundings.
- **Systems Biology** — Living things as networks of interacting parts.
- **Bioinformatics** — Computing on biological data: sequences, structures, pathways.
- **Origin of Life** — How life began from chemistry.
- **Paleontology** — Ancient life, read from fossils and ancient DNA.
- **Artificial Life** — Life-like systems made from scratch, in software, hardware or chemistry.

### Biotechnology

Engineering living things.

- **Genetic Engineering** — Changing an organism's genes on purpose.
- **Gene Editing** — Precise edits to DNA in living cells, by CRISPR and its successors.
- **Synthetic Biology** — Designing organisms and biological parts from the ground up.
- **Protein Engineering** — Designing proteins and enzymes that nature never made.
- **Metabolic Engineering** — Rewiring microbes to make chemicals, fuels and foods.
- **Biomanufacturing** — Industrial fermentation and cell culture at scale.
- **Xenobiology** — Life on altered chemistry: expanded genetic alphabets, unnatural amino acids, mirror-image cells.

### Agriculture

Growing crops and raising animals.

- **Agronomy** — The science of field crops and their yields.
- **Plant Breeding** — Developing better crop varieties.
- **Genetically Modified Crops** — Crops with engineered or edited genes.
- **Precision Agriculture** — Farming field by field and plant by plant, with sensors, data and autonomous machines.
- **Irrigation** — Bringing water to crops.
- **Soil Fertility** — Feeding soils with fertilizer, manure and microbes.
- **Crop Protection** — Defending crops against pests, weeds and disease.
- **Photosynthesis Engineering** — Making photosynthesis more efficient, as in C4 rice.
- **Animal Husbandry** — Breeding, feeding and caring for livestock.
- **Veterinary Medicine** — Medicine for animals.

### Food Production

Fish, forests and the new ways of making food.

- **Vertical Farming** — Crops grown indoors under lights, stacked high.
- **Aquaculture** — Farming fish, shellfish and seaweed.
- **Fisheries Science** — Managing wild fisheries for a sustainable catch.
- **Forestry** — Growing and harvesting forests.
- **Cellular Agriculture** — Meat and other animal products grown from cells.
- **Precision Fermentation** — Microbes engineered to make proteins and fats for food.
- **Algaculture** — Farming algae for food, feed and fuel.
- **Food Science** — Processing, preserving and packaging food.
- **Food Synthesis** — Food made from air, water and electricity, by microbes and chemistry, without crops.

## Health

Medicine and public health: keeping people well, curing them, and extending and changing what a body can do.

### Clinical Medicine

Diagnosing and treating patients.

- **Clinical Medicine** — Diagnosing and treating disease in patients.
- **Surgery** — Treating by operating on the body.
- **Robotic Surgery** — Surgery by robots, guided by surgeons near or far, or on their own.
- **Oncology** — Understanding and treating cancer.
- **Medical Imaging** — Seeing inside the body: X-ray, ultrasound, CT, MRI and PET.
- **Diagnostics** — Tests that tell what is wrong, from blood work to liquid biopsy.
- **Telemedicine** — Care delivered at a distance.
- **Precision Medicine** — Treatment fitted to each patient's genes and condition.
- **Nursing** — The practice of care: tending patients, the frail and the old.

### Pharmaceuticals

Finding, making and using medicines.

- **Pharmacology** — How drugs act on the body.
- **Drug Discovery** — Finding new medicines, now with AI and automated labs.
- **Vaccines** — Training the immune system ahead of disease.
- **Antimicrobials** — Antibiotics, antivirals and phage therapy against resistant infection.
- **RNA Therapeutics** — Medicines made of RNA: mRNA vaccines, RNA interference and more.
- **Biologics** — Medicines made by living cells, such as antibodies.

### Regenerative Medicine

Repairing and replacing cells, tissues and organs.

- **Stem Cell Therapy** — Treating disease with stem cells and the tissues grown from them.
- **Gene Therapy** — Treating disease by adding, removing or editing genes in the patient.
- **Cell Therapy** — Living cells as medicine, such as engineered immune cells against cancer.
- **Tissue Engineering** — Growing replacement tissues.
- **Bioprinting** — Printing living tissue, and in time organs.
- **Xenotransplantation** — Organs from gene-edited animals transplanted into people.

### Bionics

Machines in and on the body.

- **Artificial Organs** — Machines that stand in for hearts, kidneys, lungs and other organs.
- **Bionic Prosthetics** — Powered limbs and senses controlled by the nervous system.
- **Human Augmentation** — Implants and modifications that take the body past its natural limits.

### Longevity

Slowing, halting and reversing aging, and pausing life itself.

- **Biogerontology** — The biology of aging.
- **Senolytics** — Drugs that clear the worn-out cells that accumulate with age.
- **Cellular Reprogramming** — Resetting cells to a younger state.
- **Longevity Medicine** — Clinical treatment of aging itself, to extend healthy life.
- **Cryonics** — Preserving the dead at deep cold in hope of future revival.
- **Suspended Animation** — Slowing a living body to a near halt for long voyages or emergencies.
- **Nanomedicine** — Medicine at the molecular scale, from targeted nanoparticles to medical nanorobots.

### Public Health

The health of whole populations.

- **Epidemiology** — How disease spreads through populations, and why.
- **Sanitation** — Clean water, sewers and hygiene.
- **Nutrition Science** — What food does to health.
- **Biosecurity** — Preparing for and stopping pandemics, natural or made.
- **Health Systems** — Organizing and paying for care across a society.

### Reproduction

Fertility, conception and birth.

- **Reproductive Medicine** — Fertility treatment, in vitro fertilization and prenatal care.
- **Contraception** — Preventing pregnancy.
- **Embryo Screening** — Reading embryos' genomes before implantation.
- **Artificial Wombs** — Gestation outside the body.
- **Germline Engineering** — Heritable changes to human genes.

## Mind

Brains and minds, natural and artificial: neuroscience, psychology, cognition, consciousness, and the technologies that read, change and copy minds.

### Neuroscience

The brain and nervous system, and devices that work with them.

- **Neuroscience** — How nervous systems work, from molecules to behavior.
- **Connectomics** — Mapping every connection in a brain.
- **Computational Neuroscience** — Brains as computers: models of neurons, circuits and cognition.
- **Optogenetics** — Switching neurons on and off with light.
- **Neuromodulation** — Changing brain activity with electrical, magnetic or ultrasound stimulation.
- **Brain-Computer Interfaces** — Direct links between brains and machines.

### Psychology

Behavior and mental life, and their disorders.

- **Cognitive Psychology** — Perception, memory, attention and thought.
- **Developmental Psychology** — How minds grow and change over a life.
- **Social Psychology** — How people think about, influence and relate to one another.
- **Clinical Psychology** — Assessing and treating mental distress, psychotherapy among it.
- **Psychiatry** — Medicine of the mind: diagnosing and treating mental illness.
- **Psychometrics** — Measuring abilities, traits and attitudes.
- **Wellbeing Science** — What makes lives go well: happiness and flourishing.

### Cognitive Science

The mind as a system: language, thought and consciousness.

- **Cognitive Science** — The interdisciplinary science of mind and intelligence.
- **Linguistics** — The structure, use and origins of language.
- **Consciousness Studies** — What consciousness is, and which systems have it.
- **Animal Cognition** — The minds of other animals, and decoding their communication.

### Mind Technology

Technologies that enhance, extend, connect or copy minds.

- **Cognitive Enhancement** — Raising intelligence, memory and focus by drugs, devices or genes.
- **Psychedelic Science** — Psychedelics as a probe of the mind and a treatment for its disorders.
- **Machine Consciousness** — Building machines that are conscious, and knowing whether they are.
- **Mind Uploading** — Copying a mind into a computer: whole brain emulation.
- **Brain-to-Brain Interfaces** — Minds linked directly: synthetic telepathy.
- **Collective Intelligence** — Groups, markets and networks that think better than their members.

## Society

How people organize: economics, business, government, law, the social sciences and education.

### Economics

Production, exchange, money and the choices behind them.

- **Microeconomics** — How households and firms decide, and how markets set prices.
- **Macroeconomics** — Whole economies: growth, cycles, inflation and unemployment.
- **Econometrics** — Statistical methods for economic data.
- **Behavioral Economics** — Economics of people as they really decide.
- **Development Economics** — Why some nations are rich and others poor, and what changes it.
- **Financial Economics** — Asset prices, risk and the markets for capital.
- **Monetary Economics** — Money, banking and central banks.
- **Public Finance** — Taxing, spending and transfers, a basic income among them.
- **Labor Economics** — Work, wages and employment, and what automation does to them.
- **Mechanism Design** — Designing rules, auctions and markets so that self-interest gives the outcome wanted.
- **Economic Planning** — Running an economy by calculation instead of markets, from Soviet plans and Cybersyn to computational planning.

### Business and Management

Running organizations and building new ones.

- **Management** — Leading and running organizations.
- **Accounting** — Recording, reporting and auditing what organizations own, owe and earn.
- **Marketing** — Understanding customers and reaching them.
- **Financial Engineering** — Designing financial instruments and the models that price them.
- **Actuarial Science** — Pricing risk for insurance and pensions.
- **Entrepreneurship** — Founding and growing new ventures, and the venture finance behind them.
- **Organizational Design** — How organizations are structured, and how they stay vigorous or ossify.
- **Project Management** — Delivering large projects on time and on budget.
- **Metascience** — The science of science: how research is funded, organized and judged.

### Politics and Governance

Power, government and collective decisions.

- **Political Science** — Governments, power and political behavior.
- **Public Administration** — The capacity of the state: collecting taxes and delivering public services.
- **International Relations** — Relations between states: diplomacy, alliances, war and trade.
- **Social Choice Theory** — How individual preferences can be combined into collective decisions: voting theory.
- **Deliberative Democracy** — Citizens' assemblies, sortition and other ways for the public to reason and decide together.
- **Policy Analysis** — Weighing what public policies will do before and after they are tried.
- **Propaganda** — Shaping what populations believe: persuasion and information operations.
- **Algorithmic Governance** — Government by software and AI, from digital services to automated decisions.

### Law

Rules, rights and the institutions that enforce them.

- **Legal Systems** — Courts, legal codes and the rule of law.
- **International Law** — Treaties and the law between nations.
- **Space Law** — Law beyond Earth: the Outer Space Treaty, claims and the rights to space resources.
- **Intellectual Property** — Owning knowledge: patents, copyright and trade secrets.
- **Property Law** — Who owns land and resources, and how ownership passes.
- **Corporate Law** — Companies as legal persons: forming, governing and ending them.
- **Regulatory Science** — Setting and enforcing safety rules for drugs, reactors, vehicles and AI.
- **Legal Personhood** — Who counts as a person before the law: corporations, animals, AIs and uploaded minds.

### Social Science

Societies, peoples and their histories, measured and modeled.

- **Sociology** — How societies are organized and how they change.
- **Anthropology** — Humanity across cultures and through time.
- **Demography** — Births, deaths and migration: how populations change.
- **Urban Planning** — Designing and governing cities.
- **Criminology** — Crime: its causes, prevention and punishment.
- **Computational Social Science** — Studying societies through big data and simulation.
- **Cliodynamics** — Mathematical history: modeling the rise and fall of societies.
- **Futures Studies** — Foresight: exploring and preparing for possible futures.
- **Cultural Evolution** — How culture is inherited, spread and changed.

### Education

Teaching and learning.

- **Pedagogy** — The craft of teaching.
- **Learning Sciences** — How people learn, and what helps them.
- **Educational Technology** — Software and devices for teaching and learning, online learning among them.
- **AI Tutoring** — One-to-one teaching by machines.

## Security

Defense, intelligence, public safety and planetary defense.

### Defense

Military strategy and the weapons and platforms of war.

- **Military Strategy** — Planning and fighting wars, and deterring them.
- **Nuclear Weapons** — Fission and fusion weapons, and keeping a stockpile working.
- **Missile Technology** — Guided missiles, ballistic and cruise.
- **Missile Defense** — Detecting and intercepting missiles in flight.
- **Directed-Energy Weapons** — Lasers and microwave weapons.
- **Electronic Warfare** — Jamming, deceiving and listening to the enemy's radio and radar.
- **Stealth** — Making craft hard to detect by radar, heat and sound.
- **Autonomous Weapons** — Weapons that find and engage targets on their own, drone swarms among them.
- **Anti-Satellite Weapons** — Weapons that disable or destroy satellites.
- **Cyber Warfare** — Attacking an adversary's computers and networks.
- **Kinetic Bombardment** — Dense projectiles dropped from orbit.

### Intelligence and Public Safety

Knowing what is happening, and keeping order and people safe.

- **Intelligence Analysis** — Gathering and judging secret information.
- **Surveillance** — Watching people and places at scale.
- **Forensic Science** — Science in the service of investigation and the courts.
- **Policing** — Keeping public order and enforcing the law.
- **Emergency Management** — Preparing for and responding to disasters.

### Planetary Defense

Protecting a world from impacts, and watching the space around it.

- **Asteroid Detection** — Finding and tracking asteroids and comets that could strike.
- **Asteroid Deflection** — Pushing a threatening asteroid off course.
- **Space Domain Awareness** — Tracking everything in orbit.

## Culture

Meaning, beauty and memory: philosophy, history, language, the arts, media and belief.

### Philosophy

The oldest questions: knowledge, being, value and mind.

- **Epistemology** — What knowledge is, and how we come to have it.
- **Metaphysics** — The nature of reality: being, time, cause and identity.
- **Ethics** — What is right and good, and why.
- **Bioethics** — Ethics of medicine and the life sciences.
- **Machine Ethics** — Ethics for and of artificial minds.
- **Philosophy of Science** — How science works, and what its theories tell us.
- **Philosophy of Mind** — Minds, bodies and consciousness.
- **Political Philosophy** — Justice, rights and legitimate power.
- **Aesthetics** — Beauty and art, and our judgments of them.
- **Transhumanism** — The philosophy of improving the human condition, and the human, by technology.

### History and Heritage

The human past, and keeping its record.

- **History** — Understanding the human past.
- **Archaeology** — The past read from what people left behind.
- **Cultural Heritage** — Museums, archives and the long-term keeping of what a civilization knows.

### Language and Literature

Words and stories.

- **Literature** — Writing as art: fiction, poetry and drama.
- **Translation** — Carrying meaning between languages.
- **Rhetoric** — The art of persuasion in speech and writing.

### Arts

Making beautiful, moving and useful things.

- **Visual Arts** — Painting, sculpture, photography and their descendants.
- **Music** — Composing and performing music.
- **Performing Arts** — Theater, dance and live performance.
- **Film** — Moving pictures, from cinema to streaming series.
- **Architecture** — The art and craft of buildings and places.
- **Industrial Design** — Designing manufactured things for use and beauty.
- **Game Design** — Designing games, electronic and otherwise.
- **Craftsmanship** — The hand skills of makers: woodwork, metalwork, pottery and weaving.
- **Culinary Arts** — Cooking as craft and art.
- **Sports** — Athletic games, and the training behind them.

### Media

News, broadcast and the networks where people talk.

- **Journalism** — Finding out and reporting the news.
- **Broadcasting** — Radio, television and streaming to mass audiences.
- **Social Media** — Networks where people share, talk and are shown what to see.

### Belief and Meaning

Religion, contemplation and the search for meaning.

- **Theology** — Reasoned study of the divine and of religious belief.
- **Religious Studies** — Religions as human phenomena: their histories, practices and spread.
- **Contemplative Practice** — Meditation and other disciplines of attention.
- **Cosmism** — The belief that humanity's destiny lies in space and in overcoming death, an inspiration of Russia's rocketry.

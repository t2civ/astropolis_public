# Operations (Schema)

Operations are described in `operations.descriptive.md`.


## Common Fields

- name (1st column) — Operation name per data table instructions. For extraction operations, the name is constructed with the `stratum_group` value as a prefix, e.g., `CONT_SURFACE_COAL_MINING`, `OCEAN_DESALINATION`, `ATMOSPHERE_SEPARATION`.
- module — The module that provides capacity for this operation. Each operation belongs to exactly one module (one-to-many relationship from modules to operations).
- process_group — How the operation's run is decided (`Enums.ProcessGroup`); blank disables the operation.
  - `PRODUCTION` — Every operation without mechanics of its own, of every op class. It runs at the utilization the facility sets, or a renewable-power operation at the capacity factor its body and site allow (see `unique_type`), within its margin floor, its storage and its share of the supply of its inputs: what the facility holds and makes over the interval, shared with its other operations. An operation that names a `stratum_group` extracts (see Extraction Fields).
  - `POPULATION` — Its run follows the occupancy of the housing its module provides (the population in the module's carrying-capacity group over that group's carrying capacity), whatever its margin or storage, and it draws each input, or each substitution group (`in_inventory_groups`), as far as the facility's supply allows, ahead of the other operations. Its rates are per unit of its module's housing.
  - `EXCHANGE` — Runs the forward market's clearing service at the system's trading activity, earning a share of the exchange's take.
  - `BUILDOUT`, `DECOMMISSIONING` — Build and retire the facility's modules. They list no flows.
- unique_type — Marks an operation with unique hard-coded mechanics (`Enums.UniqueOperations`); blank for ordinary operations. The renewable-power operations (SOLAR_POWER, WIND_POWER, HYDROPOWER, GEOTHERMAL_POWER, TIDAL_POWER) are tagged so engine code can resolve them by `db_find(&"operations", &"unique_type", …)` and drive their run rate from a computed environmental capacity factor (by body and site) rather than from the seeded `rate_ppt`.
- solar — Direct solar radiation power input to the operation in MW. Non-empty only for operations that directly capture and use solar radiation for their primary process: SOLAR_POWER (photovoltaic/CSP electricity generation), SOLAR_STEELMAKING (concentrated solar thermal reduction), and biological operations using photosynthesis (for which solar represents the optimized photosynthetically active radiation input; in some contexts all or part of light may instead be supplied via additional electricity). Empty (not applicable) for all other operations, including those consuming grid electricity originally generated from solar sources.
- heat — Waste heat generated by the operation in MW. The thermal power that must be dissipated (via radiators, cooling towers, or environmental sinks) or optionally recovered for facility or district heating. Derived from the energy balance as follows, with generator and E as defined under Energy Balance. For a generator: heat = E × (1 − efficiency) / efficiency. For any other operation: heat = (E + solar) × (1 − efficiency). For operations where significant process energy comes from exothermic chemical reactions among input resources (e.g., BASIC_O2_STEELMAKING, COAL_GASIFICATION), the heat value captures only waste heat from electrical and solar energy processing; thermal energy released by chemical reactions is implicitly carried by output resource streams and managed elsewhere in the simulation.
- efficiency — Energy efficiency of the operation, dimensionless, range (0, 1). The ratio of useful energy output to total tracked energy input, and so what sets the ratio between an operation's driver and target flows (see Flow Fields). For a generator: efficiency = E / total_primary_energy, where total primary energy is the full energy captured from the source (solar radiation, chemical energy of fuel, nuclear thermal power, geothermal heat, etc.). For any other operation: efficiency = useful_work / (E + solar), i.e., the fraction of electrical and solar input that performs productive work rather than generating waste heat. Select values that are somewhat forward-looking but within the range of current or near-term operations.
- civ_power — Civilization power contribution in MW for Kardashev-scale accounting. Represents the total primary energy this operation captures from natural or chemical sources, without double-counting energy already tracked at other operations. For a generator: civ_power = E / efficiency, the full primary energy harnessed. For any other operation: civ_power = solar (the direct solar energy captured independently of the electrical grid; 0 if solar is empty). Grid electricity consumed by these operations is already counted at the generating operation. The civilization's total Kardashev-scale power is the sum of civ_power across all active operations.
- target_products — Target product(s) of the operation, which is sometimes a judgment call; a generator's is ELECTRICITY. For conversion operations where the valued outputs may be unclear or context dependent, include all output resources excluding only explicit waste products such as INDUST_WASTE. For most synthesis, manufacturing, and biological operations, include only the obvious target product. Example: for OIL_REFINING, we consider as value products LIQ_HYDROCARBONS, METHANE, CARBON, and SULFUR (only excluding INDUST_WASTE).
- life_support — Inputs whose shortfall reaches the population that runs the operation; read only on a population-run operation (`process_group` `POPULATION`) and blank elsewhere. Their mass-weighted fill, smoothed over about a quarter, scales that population's effective carrying capacity, a substitution group counting as one input however its members supply it; a shortfall in the operation's other inputs is recorded only. While the residents are short of one of these inputs, the facility's operations that make it draw their own inputs alongside the residents, ahead of its other operations.
- open_cycle_storage — Array of integer-valued floats (`-1`, `0`, or `1`) giving the net storage-class direction of this operation when run in open cycle (atmospheric inputs drawn from atmosphere, gaseous/liquid waste vented to atmosphere or surface). Values are indexed by `storage_classes.tsv` rows. `1` means the op is a net producer into that storage class, `-1` means it draws from it, `0` means no effect. May be left empty for operations with no storage effects at all.
- closed_cycle_storage — Same format as `open_cycle_storage`, but for closed-cycle operation: atmospheric inputs come from inventory (cryogenic reserves, etc.), gaseous/liquid outputs route back to inventory rather than venting, and the operation's driver flows are scaled by `closed_cycle_factor` (note 14). Used when the facility or module forces closed-cycle processing.
- revenue_type — `line_items.tsv` line item this operation's (imputed) revenue books into for non-unitary facilities. A financial concept independent of `op_class` (a GUI-only grouping); leave empty for none.
- cogs_type — `line_items.tsv` line item this operation's (imputed) cost of goods books into for non-unitary facilities. Leave empty for none. A population-run operation books what its residents consume here, into an operating-expense item (`POPULATION_UPKEEP`).
- maintenance — Resources consumed as rate-based maintenance, scaling with the operation's run (capacity × utilization). Parallel to `maintenance_rates`. Unlike stoichiometric inputs, maintenance is deferrable: a shortfall accrues to the facility's deferred maintenance rather than halting the operation. Applies across all process groups (renewables and extractions included) and books to the `MAINTENANCE` line item, not `cogs_type`.
- maintenance_rates — Maintenance resource rates (per unit of capacity × utilization), parallel to `maintenance`.


## Extraction Fields

A `PRODUCTION` operation that names a `stratum_group` extracts from that stratum, and the other fields here are read only for such an operation.

- stratum_group — Target stratum_group. See `stratum_group` in strata.tsv.
- target_deposits — List of resources that the operation specifically targets as deposits. If this field is blank, then the operation extracts all volatile resources present.
- target_rate — Expected extraction rate of target deposit resource(s) in t/h per unit run, which is 1 MW of electricity driver input (see Normalization), at optimal deposit level (see note 1). Sum of all target deposit resources for operations with multiple targets.
- overburden — Mass rate of overburden generated. For mining and drilling operations, this is typically STONE and REGOLITH removed from the target stratum and converted to REGOLITH at the extraction site.
- discovered_norm — Normalizing `discovered` level (0 < value ≤ 1.0) for which `target_rate`, the driver flows, and `overburden` are defined. Corresponds to optimal resource deposit conditions per note 1. For operations with multiple target deposits, this is the sum of individual `discovered` values. The `discovered` value for each resource in each stratum is calculated per the formula in strata_resources.schema.md (Practical Effect of Dispersion). Actual `target_rate` scales linearly: if the actual `discovered` in a stratum is half `discovered_norm`, the effective `target_rate` is half the table value at the same driver flows and throughput. Note: `discovered_norm` may exceed the formula-computed maximum for a stratum_group to ensure that per-discovered-unit productivity (`target_rate / discovered_norm`) decreases monotonically with depth.


## Flow Fields

An operation's flows are the resources it takes in and puts out, each in one of two roles:

- **Driver** flows are the energy the operation runs on and what that source becomes: the electricity a consumer draws, or a generator's fuel, the oxygen it burns and its combustion or fission products. They scale with the operation's run rate, and `closed_cycle_factor` multiplies them in closed cycle.
- **Target** flows are what the operation processes or produces: a consumer's feedstock and products, or a generator's electricity. They scale with its effective rate.

A flow is a driver if its ratio to the operation's products would change with the operation's energy efficiency. Efficiency sets the ratio between the two roles, and at runtime the effective rate over the run rate modifies it. An operation that runs on a different energy source, such as fuel in place of electricity, is a separate operation on the same module with its own driver flows; local economics decide which one runs. A generator is an operation whose target output is ELECTRICITY.

Every process group except BUILDOUT and DECOMMISSIONING lists its flows here. An extraction lists its driver flows, and its deposits (`target_deposits`, or the volatiles present) are implicit target outputs. A renewable lists only its target `out_inventory` (note 2). All lists are semicolon-delimited, and each rates list is parallel to its resource list.

- driver_in_inventory, driver_in_atmos, driver_out_inventory, driver_out_atmos, driver_out_surface — Driver flows, each with a `_rates` column: the same streams and formats as the target columns below.
- in_inventory — Input resources from inventory.
- in_inventory_rates — Input resource rates (t/h) from inventory.
- in_inventory_groups — Substitution groups, parallel to `in_inventory`; driver flows have none. Inputs sharing a positive id are interchangeable one for one in their unit; 0, or a blank cell, leaves an input on its own. The operation draws a group's combined rate from whichever members are supplied, covering a short member from what is left of the others once every operation's own need is met, in proportion to that spare, so only the group as a whole limits the run, and each member still signals demand for at least its own rate. Use a group for feed whose substitution leaves the outputs unchanged, such as the foods a household eats or the materials a food plant packages with; an alternative that changes the process, its energy or its outputs is a separate operation on the same module. A group has two or more members, all goods with a storage class or all in one unit, none of them also an `in_atmos` input, and on a population-run operation all or none of them in `life_support`. Only `PRODUCTION` and `POPULATION` operations take groups.
- in_atmos — Input resources from atmosphere.
- in_atmos_rates — Input resource rates (t/h) from atmosphere.
- out_inventory — Output resources to inventory.
- out_inventory_rates — Output resource rates (t/h) to inventory.
- out_atmos — Output resources to atmosphere.
- out_atmos_rates — Output resource rates (t/h) to atmosphere.
- out_surface — Output resources to surface. Often WATER.
- out_surface_rates — Output resource rates (t/h) to surface.
- closed_cycle_factor — Energy multiplier factor to run operation as closed cycle, i.e., all inputs and outputs are from and to inventory. It multiplies the driver flows (note 14).
- mass_flow — Equals the sum of the mass input rates equals the sum of the mass output rates, within rounding precision: of the target flows, or of the driver flows for a generator. Note: the column header in `operations.tsv` is currently `mass_conversion`; the name discrepancy will be resolved in a future update.
- fuel_rate — Generators only. Total driver input rate excluding OXYGEN.

Rate units: the `*_rates` columns carry a default unit of `t/h`, which is correct for mass resources. For a non-mass resource (e.g., electricity in `MW`, compute in `Zflop`, or a service whose `trade_unit` is `1`) write each rate element with an explicit unit suffix so it overrides the column default — for example `1.88 MW` for electricity, `3.6 Zflop/h` for compute output, or `1/h` for one service unit per hour. A bare number on a non-mass resource is silently converted as `t/h` and will be wrong by the resource's unit multiplier. The engine multiplies a rate by the resource's sim-unit price (`start_price ÷ trade_unit multiplier`), so revenue/cost equals `quantity × start_price` regardless of unit; only the explicit suffix keeps the quantity coherent.


## Normalization

When not otherwise constrained, prefer normalization of generators to 1 MW of electricity output, extraction operations to 1 MW of electricity driver input, and other non-service operations to 1 t/h of mass throughput. Adjust as needed when implementing modules that enable multiple operations.



## Notes

1. For extraction operations (those with a `stratum_group`) with `target_deposits`, extraction and energy values assume optimal deposit level and knowledge of the target resource(s). This is a best case scenario within the range of realistic possibility.
2. Renewable power operations and RADIOISOTOPE_POWER list only their electricity output.
3. For generators, normalize the operation to 1 MW of electricity output. For others, normalize the operation so that `mass_flow` is 1 t/h.
4. Use resources as they are defined in physical_resources.md. E.g., input rate for LE_URANIUM_FUEL should consider the assembled fuel weight, not just the uranium content.
5. Use catchall resources as needed. E.g., if an operation in the real-world requires 1 kg/h of lithium, use 1 kg/h of INDUSTRIAL_METALS.
6. For chemical conversions, consider only stoichiometric quantities for inputs and outputs. For fabricated/manufactured items, consider product composition and subtractive processes (the latter producing INDUSTRIAL_WASTE, REGOLITH, etc.).
7. Don't consider plant maintenance or repair parts used to run the operation; that will be handled separately.
8. Consider inputs and outputs as a net reaction within each stream, driver and target flows together. A resource appears at most once among an operation's inventory inputs (`driver_in_inventory` and `in_inventory`), at most once among its inventory outputs (`out_inventory` and `driver_out_inventory`), and never in both. Resources may appear in different streams (e.g., WATER from `in_inventory` and WATER to `out_surface`).
9. Operation energy including process heat should usually come from an electricity driver input rather than other sources such as fuel combustion (this is a model simplification). An operation run on fuel instead is a sibling operation on the same module, with the fuel, the oxygen it burns and the combustion products as its driver flows. Exceptions: cases where energy is derived directly from operation inputs, such as CARBON and OXYGEN in BASIC_O2_STEELMAKING (in this case electricity accounts for auxiliary requirements); cases where direct solar energy is explicitly used for heat (tracked in the solar column).
10. Include industrial process water (washing, quenching, slurry transport, wet processing) as WATER input from inventory and output to surface. Do not include cooling water for thermal management (covered by the heat/efficiency model). Include stoichiometric water as before.
11. Define operations as "open" by assigning atmospheric inputs (`in_atmos`) and atmospheric/surface outputs (`out_atmos`, `out_surface`) where the open-cycle form is the natural baseline; a driver flow takes the same streams in the `driver_` columns. Oxygen consumed in combustion and similar reactions should come from atmosphere (`in_atmos`); oxygen for a process that nitrogen would spoil -- oxygen-blown gasification, basic-oxygen steelmaking, autothermal reforming, chemical partial oxidation, semiconductor processing -- is purified oxygen from an air separation unit and belongs in `in_inventory`, where the operation pays for it. The test is whether the process tolerates nitrogen, not whether oxygen is consumed. A process run on enriched air, such as a blast furnace, takes its enrichment share as purified oxygen in `in_inventory` and the rest from the atmosphere. Gaseous waste products (CO₂, water vapor) should go to atmosphere (`out_atmos`). Industrial wastewater should go to surface (`out_surface`). The `closed_cycle_factor` captures the energy multiplier for running fully closed. For operations where the open/closed difference is negligible, keep the operation fully closed (`closed_cycle_factor` = 1).
12. Be forward looking if the particular operation is not well constrained (as stated above for efficiency).
13. Report if you find major gaps in the operations list or associated lists.
14. The `closed_cycle_factor` is applied as a simple multiplier to the operation's driver flows and `heat`: a consumer draws more electricity, and a generator burns more fuel for the same electricity output. It accounts for energy to: (a) obtain atmospheric inputs from cryogenic/solid inventory, (b) capture and process gaseous outputs for inventory storage, (c) purify and recycle wastewater instead of discharging to surface. Approximate energy costs per kg for closing streams: O₂ cryogenic handling ~0.25 MJ/kg, CO₂ capture/storage ~0.20 MJ/kg, H₂O vapor condensation/freeze ~0.10 MJ/kg, N₂ cryogenic handling ~0.25 MJ/kg, process water freeze/thaw cycle ~0.10 MJ/kg.


## Energy Balance

All non-extraction operations satisfy the following energy relationships. A generator is an operation whose target output is ELECTRICITY (see Flow Fields), and E is an operation's electricity rate in MW: a generator's output, or any other operation's electricity driver input (0 if it has none).

Generators: The operation captures primary energy from a natural or chemical source and converts a fraction equal to efficiency into electricity. The remainder is waste heat. Identities: heat = E × (1 − efficiency) / efficiency; civ_power = E / efficiency = E + heat.

Other operations: The operation uses electrical and/or direct solar energy, converting a fraction equal to efficiency into useful work. The remainder is waste heat. Identities: heat = (E + solar) × (1 − efficiency); civ_power = solar (or 0 if not applicable).

Verification: for a generator, efficiency = E / (E + heat). For any other operation, efficiency = 1 − heat / (E + solar).
